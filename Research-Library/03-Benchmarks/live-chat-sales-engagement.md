Products in scope so far: Zoho SalesIQ (anchor, primary product record, Layer 1–2 researched) plus two stub competitor records, LiveChat and tawk.to. ## Cross-Category Note: Overlap with Customer Support / Helpdesk
Live chat and helpdesk/ticketing are adjacent, overlapping categories rather than cleanly separated ones. **Intercom** is filed under Customer Support / Helpdesk (`../02-Competitor-Products/customer-support-helpdesk/intercom.md`) because its core product is a unified support inbox, but its live-chat widget and proactive-messaging capability compete directly with Zoho SalesIQ, LiveChat, and tawk.to in the pre-sale/on-site engagement use case. Likewise, **Zoho Desk** (Customer Support / Helpdesk) consumes **Zoho SalesIQ** as its live-chat channel provider at the Enterprise tier (see `../01-Zoho-Primary-Products/zoho-desk.md` Section 3 and `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 1) — meaning within Zoho's own product line, "Live Chat & Sales Engagement" and "Customer Support / Helpdesk" are not competing categories but complementary, integrated ones. Any benchmark or scoring exercise across these two categories should treat Intercom as a shared/cross-listed competitor rather than double-counting it as an independent data point in both benchmarks.

## 3. What Each Competitor Does Better Than the Others
- **Zoho SalesIQ:** Native, bidirectional Zoho CRM/Desk/Campaigns integration with automatic lead scoring and chat-to-CRM push; visitor behavior correlation view (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, see `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 5).
- **LiveChat:** Polished, mature UI/UX with the highest and most-substantiated headline customer-satisfaction rating in this category (Capterra 4.7/5, ~1,649 reviews); consistently praised onboarding and support-team responsiveness (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, see `livechat.md` Section 5).
- **tawk.to:** Unmatched cost-accessibility — a genuinely free, unlimited-agent core product, positioned as the default choice for cash-constrained SMBs/startups (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, see `tawk-to.md` Section 2/5).
- **Drift (historical, pre-sunset):** Was the category's enterprise/conversational-marketing leader with the deepest AI-driven meeting-booking and pipeline-generation focus, but this is no longer a live competitive advantage given the 2026 sunset (see Section 1 note).
- **Intercom (cross-listed):** See `../02-Competitor-Products/customer-support-helpdesk/intercom.md` Section 3 — modern UI and the Fin AI agent, offset by the category's most consistently criticized pricing model.

## 4. Patterns to Adopt
- Native, bidirectional CRM integration (auto-push chats as scored leads, surface existing-customer status inside the chat widget) — the single most consistently praised Zoho SalesIQ feature (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 5) and structurally similar to the "unified omnichannel dashboard" pattern already confirmed as a cross-category best practice in the Customer Support/Helpdesk benchmark (`customer-support-helpdesk.md` Section 4) — reinforces that deep, native (not just webhook-level) integration between engagement tools and CRM/support systems is valued across both adjacent categories.
- A genuinely free, uncapped tier (not just a limited trial or seat-capped free plan) is the strongest acquisition lever identified for the SMB/startup segment in this category — tawk.to's free-forever model draws the most enthusiastic praise of any pricing approach gathered across all three researched products (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, `tawk-to.md` Section 5).
- Investing specifically in onboarding smoothness and support-team responsiveness appears to produce a measurable satisfaction gap — LiveChat's Capterra Customer Service-adjacent praise and overall 4.7/5 rating stand out against Zoho SalesIQ's lower Capterra Customer Service sub-score (4.1/5) (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, cross-referenced `livechat.md` Section 5 vs. `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 5).

## 5. Patterns to Avoid
- Hiding all paid-tier numeric pricing behind a "contact sales" wall — Zoho SalesIQ's official pricing page discloses plan features/limits but no paid-tier prices, making independent price-to-value comparison difficult; contrast with tawk.to's fully transparent model and LiveChat's published (if criticized) per-seat tiers (derived from Section 2 of all three product records).
- Unbundling previously-included functionality into a new, separately-priced product — LiveChat's move to split ticketing out of its live-chat plan into a standalone "HelpDesk" product is the most consistently criticized structural pricing change gathered in this category (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, `livechat.md` Section 5) — directly analogous to the Zendesk/Intercom "pricing unpredictability" pattern already flagged as a cross-product pain point in the Customer Support/Helpdesk benchmark.
- Marketing an AI chatbot as trainable on a company's own knowledge base without qualifying real-world accuracy limits — Zoho SalesIQ's Zobot draws complaints of being "misleading" on this exact point, echoing the same marketed-vs-real-world AI-capability gap already documented for Intercom's Fin agent in the adjacent Customer Support/Helpdesk benchmark (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK/INFERENCE, `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 10 and 15; cross-reference `customer-support-helpdesk.md` Section 5).
- Requiring active/logged-in dashboard presence to receive chat notifications, with no confirmed passive/offline alerting path — a specific, narrow but avoidable UX gap called out for tawk.to (CUSTOMER FEEDBACK, `tawk-to.md` Section 5).
- Building a product strategy around continuity of a single vendor's roadmap without watching for ownership-change risk — Drift's 2026 sunset following its 2024 Salesloft acquisition is now a direct, realized example (not just a "risk flag" as in Intercom's still-pending Salesforce deal) of the ownership-change pattern already noted as a cross-category watch item in the Customer Support/Helpdesk benchmark (`customer-support-helpdesk.md` Section 6) — worth treating as a confirmed instance rather than a hypothetical going forward.

## 6. Customer Pain Points Across the Market
- AI chatbot accuracy/training reliability — a recurring theme across at least two of the three researched products (Zoho SalesIQ's Zobot; LiveChat's chatbot add-on, "frustrating to use") — not yet confirmed for tawk.to's AI Assist, which has no gathered sentiment data. Echoes the same pattern already documented for Intercom's Fin in the adjacent Customer Support/Helpdesk category.
- Pricing opacity or unpredictability — confirmed via two different mechanisms: Zoho SalesIQ (no disclosed paid-tier pricing at all) and LiveChat (rising per-seat costs plus feature unbundling). tawk.to is the clear exception with a transparent free core, though its layered paid-add-on pricing has not been independently fetched from the official site in this pass.
- Reliability/connection complaints — present in scattered form for both Zoho SalesIQ and LiveChat, but each is currently only a single-data-point or low-volume signal; not yet a confirmed category-wide pattern.
- Cross-application integration friction outside a vendor's own ecosystem — noted for Zoho SalesIQ ("not all applications integrate smoothly for collaboration" — see `zoho-salesiq.md` Section 5); not yet checked for LiveChat or tawk.to.
